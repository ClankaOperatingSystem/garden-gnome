# The voice of a garden

This doctrine applies to the prose in a garden's Org files: headings,
task bodies, and the pages of projects and responsibilities. It does not
apply to quotations, code or sealed records.

## Words

**PROSE-1. Plain register.** Write as one engineer to another. State
facts. Do not persuade.

**PROSE-2. Literal names.** Name a thing with its exact noun. Do not use
a figure of speech for a thing that has a name.

**PROSE-3. One name for one thing.** In a file, give each thing one name.

**PROSE-4. One verb for one action.** In a file, use one verb for each
action.

**PROSE-5. Verbs for actions.** Write "check the log", not "perform a
check of the log".

**PROSE-6. No phrasal verbs.** Write "start", not "spin up". Write
"read", not "dive into".

**PROSE-7. Exact properties.** Name the exact property: accurate,
complete, current. Use "honest" only where the subject could lie.

**PROSE-8. Marketing words.** Do not use "robust", "seamless",
"powerful", "elegant", "comprehensive" or "cutting-edge". Describe the
property instead.

**PROSE-9. Slop words.** Do not use "leverage", "utilise", "delve",
"landscape", "journey", "holistic", "unlock" or "empower". Use the word
they replaced.

**PROSE-10. Padding.** Write "to", not "in order to". Write "because",
not "due to the fact that". Write "can", not "has the ability to".

**PROSE-11. Filler hedges.** Do not use "arguably", "quite", "somewhat"
or "it is worth noting".

**PROSE-12. Things do not want.** A program does not want, feel or
decide. Say what it does.

## Sentences

**PROSE-13. Sentence length.** A sentence has 25 words or fewer.

**PROSE-14. Instruction length.** An instruction has 20 words or fewer.

**PROSE-15. One instruction to a sentence.** Each sentence gives one
instruction.

**PROSE-16. Active voice.** Name the actor. Use the passive only when
the actor is unknown or does not matter.

**PROSE-17. Simple tenses.** Use the simple present, past and future,
and the imperative. Use the present perfect only when the present
result is the point.

**PROSE-18. Modality kept.** Keep each claim at the certainty it has. A
rewrite does not change "may have failed" to "failed".

**PROSE-19. No dropped words.** Keep the subject, the verb and the
article.

**PROSE-20. Noun clusters.** Put no more than three nouns in a row.

**PROSE-21. Garden paths.** Put the article on the object. Put the
modifiers before the noun. Keep "that" before a clause. The test is the
opening words: they must not form a phrase that the sentence does not mean.

**PROSE-22. No semicolons.** Write two sentences.

**PROSE-23. No em dashes.** Use a colon, a comma or a full stop.

**PROSE-24. Semantic line breaks.** Put one sentence or one clause on
each line. No policy judges this clause.

## Paragraphs and pages

**PROSE-25. One topic to a paragraph.** A paragraph has one topic and
six sentences or fewer.

**PROSE-26. Lists and tables.** A sequence of three or more steps is a
list. A comparison across more than two dimensions is a table. Do not
put prose in bullets to make it look organised.

**PROSE-27. Say it once.** Put the point first. Do not repeat it.

**PROSE-28. The present only.** A page says what a thing is. A task says
where work stands. Pages do not use "not yet", "for now" or "planned".

**PROSE-29. No rulings by date or person.** State the rule and its
reason. Do not say who decided it or when.

**PROSE-30. Names before ids.** Name the thing, then give the id or
link. A link's description names what the link points to.

**PROSE-31. Task headings.** A task heading starts with an imperative
verb and names its object.

## Habits of machine prose

**PROSE-32. Aphorism.** Do not write a sentence for its rhythm. The
test: restate the sentence as a plain fact. If that fact is already in
the text, or no fact can be made from it, delete the sentence.

**PROSE-33. Manufactured antithesis.** Do not write "X, not Y" when
striking "not Y" loses no fact.

**PROSE-34. Rhetorical triples.** Each item in a list states a separate
fact.

**PROSE-35. Emphasis.** Do not use bold for emphasis in running prose.
Do not use exclamation marks, capitals for emphasis or emoji.

**PROSE-36. No narration.** Say what a thing is or what was done. Do not
tell the story of arriving at it.

**PROSE-37. No self-praise.** Do not call your own work "much cleaner"
or "a solid foundation".

**PROSE-38. No questions to the reader.** A heading is not a question. A
question mark belongs only in an open question that names what would
settle it.
